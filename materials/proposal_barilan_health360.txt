About Health 360

The Health 360 Center is a multidisciplinary research center founded and led by
Professor Nadav Davidovitch at Bar-Ilan University. Health 360 serves as an innovation
hub for advancing health and social responsibility through systems thinking,
interdisciplinary research, and cross-sector collaboration.

Through research clusters spanning multiple faculties, the Center develops innovative
responses to pressing public health and societal challenges, including longevity,
diabetes and obesity, addiction, mental health, and climate change.

The Center has an existing partnership and teaching experience with the Association of
Schools of Public Health – European Region (ASPHER) as well as with the Israeli
Ministry of Health and the Israeli Health Funds.

Program Overview

The Professional Certificate in Longevity and Lifelong Wellbeing Advisory Practice is a
structured professional-training and certification program for professionals who want to
expand their existing field of practice through a specialization in longevity and wellbeing.
It prepares participants to apply evidence-informed principles with individuals, groups,
workplaces, and communities within a clearly defined non-clinical advisory scope.

Rather than functioning as a collection of independent courses, the program is designed
as a coherent professional-training sequence. Participants progress from foundational
scientific knowledge to evidence appraisal, practical skills, supervised application, and
demonstration of competence through a practicum-based capstone portfolio.

The program combines academic knowledge from Bar-Ilan University and Health 360
with accessible instruction, practical tools, case-based learning, and real-world
application.
Participation Pathways

The program will be offered through two complementary pathways:

   ● Full certification pathway: A coherent professional sequence for eligible
     participants that includes all six courses, assessment, supervised practice, a
     capstone portfolio, and final evaluation. Successful completion leads to the
     professional certificate and proposed graduate designation.
   ● Standalone enrichment courses: Individual subject courses available to the
     general public and to professionals seeking focused continuing education without
     committing to the full certification pathway.

Subject to Bar-Ilan Akadima policy, participants who complete eligible standalone
courses and later meet the admission requirements for the certification pathway may
apply to have those courses recognized toward the full program. Additional
assessments, practicum requirements, or time limits may apply.

Target Audience

The full certification pathway is intended primarily for professionals who want to broaden
their existing work through a specialization in longevity and wellbeing. Relevant
participants may include:

   ● Health promotion and public health professionals
   ● Nurses and other professionals working in health systems

   ● Welfare, employee wellbeing, and human resources managers in organizations

   ● Professionals and program staff in local authorities

   ● Professionals working in elder care organizations (community based and
     residential)

   ● Professionals working in nonprofit, civil-society, and community organizations

   ● Other qualified professionals whose current role includes health, wellbeing,
     education, workforce development, or community programming

Recommended admission requirements for the certification pathway include a relevant
academic degree or recognized professional qualification. Applicants with substantial
relevant experience may be admitted following individual review. All certification
participants must commit to the attendance, assessment, practicum, and professional-
conduct requirements and acknowledge the program’s scope of practice.

The standalone enrichment-course pathway is open to members of the general public
who want evidence-informed knowledge for personal learning, as well as professionals
seeking focused enrichment. Participation in standalone courses does not by itself
qualify an individual for the graduate professional designation.

Professional Designation and Scope of Practice

Graduates who meet all academic, practical, assessment, and conduct requirements
will be eligible to use the proposed designation Certified Longevity and Lifelong
Wellbeing Advisor, subject to final institutional approval.

The certificate documents successful completion of an academically grounded
professional program in evidence appraisal, longevity and wellbeing principles,
structured non-diagnostic assessment, planning, behavior-change support,
implementation, progress review, ethical practice, and referral. It is intended to add a
defined advisory specialization to a graduate’s existing professional role, or allow them
to take on an independent coaching or advisory practice.

The certificate does not confer a medical, nutritional, psychological, or other regulated
clinical license. Graduates may not diagnose disease, prescribe treatment, alter
medication, or provide services reserved for licensed professionals unless separately
qualified. The final credential title and scope must be approved by Bar-Ilan University’s
academic and legal authorities before launch.

Potential Professional Applications

Within their existing qualifications, role, and the program’s approved non-clinical scope,
graduates may apply their training in the following ways:

   ● Within organizations: Lead or contribute to wellbeing and health-promotion
     initiatives; assess employee or community needs; develop, implement, and
     review evidence-informed programs; advise teams on applying healthy-longevity
     principles; and help embed wellbeing into organizational practice and culture.
   ● With individuals: Conduct a structured, non-diagnostic longevity and wellbeing
     assessment; co-develop a realistic evidence-informed plan; support goal setting
     and behavior change; review progress; and recognize situations that require
     referral to a licensed professional.
Value for Employers and Organizations

For employers, sponsoring staff members builds internal capacity to turn longevity and
wellbeing evidence into practical initiatives. Graduates can help organizations:

   ● Identify workforce or community health and wellbeing needs and priorities
   ● Design and implement evidence-informed health-promotion and wellbeing
     programs

   ● Evaluate progress, refine initiatives, and communicate results to stakeholders

   ● Coordinate cross-functional initiatives and make appropriate referrals when
     specialist input is needed

   ● Strengthen an organizational culture of wellbeing and healthy longevity

Program Goals

By the end of the program, participants will be able to:

   1. Explain central biological, behavioral, emotional, and social factors associated
      with longevity and healthy aging.
   2. Distinguish between evidence-based longevity practices and claims that lack
      sufficient scientific support.
   3. Evaluate the relationships between nutrition, sleep, stress, behavior, purpose,
      and social connection.
   4. Apply practical tools for supporting lifelong health and wellbeing.
   5. Analyze longevity challenges through a multidisciplinary and systems-based
      perspective.
   6. Develop an evidence-informed plan, intervention, or educational resource
      addressing a defined longevity or wellbeing need.

   7. Conduct and document a structured, non-diagnostic longevity and wellbeing
      assessment.

   8. Co-create an individualized longevity and wellbeing plan with measurable goals
      and an appropriate follow-up process.

   9. Apply behavior-change and communication tools in individual or group settings.
   10. Maintain professional boundaries, recognize red flags, and make appropriate
       referrals.

Pedagogical Approach

The program will be:

   ● Evidence-based but accessible
   ● Structured as a progressive learning journey
   ● Interdisciplinary, integrating science, society, and real-world implementation
   ● Focused on practical tools and personal or professional application
   ● Based on active participation, reflection, skills practice, supervised casework,
     and project-based learning
   ● Supported by a Health 360 knowledge library containing videos, readings, case
     studies, and additional resources

Each course will build on knowledge and tools introduced in the previous courses. A
continuous professional-practice strand will address structured intake, non-diagnostic
assessment, goal setting, behavior change, ethical practice, documentation,
professional boundaries, red flags, and referral. Participants will develop their
supervised practice case and capstone portfolio progressively throughout the program.

Delivery: Full Certification Pathway

   ● Synchronous online instruction delivered by trained instructors from the eTeacher
     team
   ● A defined sequence of six courses, with four synchronous 90-minute lessons in
     each course
   ● Approximately 30 hours of guided independent learning through the Health 360
     digital knowledge library
   ● Short assignments and applied learning activities between courses
   ● Instructor feedback and professional evaluation
   ● Three guided practicum and capstone clinics, followed by two final presentation
     and oral-assessment sessions

The proposed pilot includes 30 synchronous lessons of 90 minutes each: 24 lessons
across the six subject courses, one orientation and professional-scope lesson, three
practicum and capstone clinics, and two final assessment sessions. This equals 45
clock hours or 60 academic hours of live instruction. Together with approximately 30
hours of guided independent learning and 25 hours of practicum and capstone work, the
total learning load is approximately 100 clock hours. The program will run over 30
teaching weeks and will normally be completed in approximately eight months,
excluding institutional breaks.

Standalone Enrichment Courses

Members of the general public and professionals seeking focused continuing education
may enroll in individual subject courses without undertaking the practicum, capstone
portfolio, or final professional assessment required for certification.

Successful completion of a standalone course may lead to a course-specific certificate
of completion or participation, subject to Bar-Ilan Akadima policy. It does not confer the
professional certificate or the graduate designation. Any later recognition toward the full
certification pathway will depend on admission eligibility, satisfactory assessment,
course recency, and institutional approval.

Program Structure

Stage 1: Scientific Foundations

Course 1: The Science of Longevity – What Actually Extends Life?

Participants will examine the scientific foundations of longevity and healthy aging. The
course will introduce major biological processes, population-level findings, risk and
protective factors, and the distinction between established evidence and emerging or
unsupported longevity claims.

Stage 2: Health Behaviors and Daily Practice

Course 2: Food as Medicine – Nutrition for Lifelong Health

Participants will explore the relationship between food, chronic disease prevention,
functional health, and healthy aging. The course will emphasize evidence-based
nutritional principles and their practical application in different personal, cultural, and
socioeconomic contexts. The issue of health inequities in access to food and food
culture will be central.

Course 3: Habits That Shape Health – Behavior Change and Longevity

Participants will examine how habits are formed, maintained, and changed. They will
learn practical behavior-change tools and consider how individual choices are shaped
by family, community, environment, and wider social systems, including ingrained
inequality in social structures.

Course 4: Sleep, Stress, and Healthy Aging

Participants will study the roles of sleep, stress, recovery, and physiological regulation
in lifelong health. They will assess common barriers to healthy routines and develop
realistic strategies for improving sleep and stress management.

Stage 3: Emotional and Social Wellbeing

Course 5: Purpose, Meaning, and Emotional Wellbeing

Participants will explore the relationship between emotional wellbeing, purpose,
resilience, identity, life transitions, and healthy aging. The course will include reflective
tools that can be applied personally or in professional and community settings.

Course 6: Social Connection and Longevity

Participants will examine social connection as a central dimension of health and
longevity. Topics will include loneliness, belonging, family and community relationships,
social participation, and the development of supportive environments across the life
course.

Stage 4: Integration, Supervised Practicum, and Capstone Assessment

Following the six courses, participants will integrate the program’s scientific, behavioral,
emotional, and social perspectives through at least one supervised practice case and a
capstone portfolio.

Two concluding sessions will allow participants to present and defend their work,
receive feedback, and demonstrate their readiness to apply the program’s principles
within the approved scope of practice.

Learning and Assessment Framework

Participant achievement will be evaluated throughout the program rather than through
attendance alone.
Assessment will include:

   ● Participation in synchronous sessions, structured discussions, and skills
     laboratories
   ● Short knowledge checks or quizzes
   ● Applied assignments and case-based skills assessments connected to the
     individual courses
   ● Observed or recorded skills assessment
   ● Completion of a supervised practice case
   ● Progressive development of the practicum and capstone portfolio
   ● Final capstone submission, presentation, and oral assessment

Suggested Assessment Weighting

   ●   Participation, engagement, and professional conduct: 10%
   ●   Course knowledge checks: 20%
   ●   Applied assignments and case-based skills assessments: 30%
   ●   Supervised practicum and capstone portfolio: 30%

   ● Final presentation and oral assessment: 10%

The precise weighting may be adjusted according to Bar-Ilan Akadima’s academic and
certification requirements.

Supervised Practicum and Capstone Portfolio

Each participant will complete at least one full supervised practice case in an approved
live, simulated, or workplace-based format. The case must demonstrate the participant’s
ability to move responsibly from assessment to planning, implementation, review, and
referral when relevant.

The capstone portfolio will include:

   ● A structured intake and non-diagnostic longevity and wellbeing assessment
   ● A clearly defined client or target-population profile and longevity-related need
   ● An evidence summary drawing on credible sources and content from at least four
     courses
   ● A collaborative, prioritized longevity and wellbeing plan with measurable
     objectives
   ● Documentation of implementation, follow-up, feedback, and any modifications
     made
   ● Identification of risks, ethical issues, professional limits, and referral
     considerations
   ● A reflective analysis of the advisor’s decisions, challenges, learning, and next
     steps

Each portfolio must also:

   1. Identify a clearly defined need, challenge, or target population.
   2. Incorporate knowledge from at least four of the program’s six courses.
   3. Refer to credible scientific or professional sources.
   4. Present realistic and actionable recommendations.
   5. Address relevant barriers, limitations, and ethical considerations.
   6. Include documented implementation, follow-up, or an approved simulated
      equivalent.

Portfolios will be evaluated according to a standardized rubric assessing scientific
accuracy, assessment and planning skills, professional judgment, ethical practice,
communication, feasibility, documentation, reflection, and quality of presentation.

Conditions for Certification

To receive the Professional Certificate in Longevity and Lifelong Wellbeing Advisory
Practice, participants must:

   ● Attend at least 85% of synchronous sessions and all required practicum and
     capstone clinics, unless an approved alternative is completed
   ● Complete all required course assignments
   ● Achieve at least 70% in each course and an overall program grade of at least
     75%
   ● Successfully complete the supervised practice case and receive approval for the
     capstone portfolio
   ● Pass the final presentation and oral assessment
   ● Demonstrate appropriate academic and professional conduct

Participants who attend the program but do not meet all certification requirements may
receive a certificate of participation, subject to Bar-Ilan Akadima policy. They may not
use the graduate professional designation.
Academic and Professional Quality Assurance

To maintain the program’s premium and academically grounded character:

   ● An academic director appointed or approved by Bar-Ilan Akadima and Health
     360 will hold responsibility for the syllabus, professional scope, assessment
     standards, and certification decisions. Core content will be developed or
     approved by Health 360 subject-matter experts.
   ● eTeacher instructors will receive program-specific training and teaching
     materials.
   ● Participants will receive clear learning objectives, assignment instructions, and
     assessment criteria.
   ● Standardized practicum, capstone, and observed-skills rubrics will be used
     across cohorts, and supervisors and assessors will meet qualification criteria
     approved by the academic director.
   ● Participant feedback and achievement data will be reviewed after each cohort.
   ● Course materials will be periodically updated to reflect new evidence and
     developments in longevity research.
